Hosted privacy notice · Updated 24 October 2026

Your agents.
Your data. Your choice.

Here is what hosted Engram stores, who receives it, and how to ask us to change or delete it.

Who operates Engram

Outcome Driven Holdings LLC operates the hosted service at engram.ani.computer and is responsible for its handling of your information. Contact ani@odstdio.com for privacy requests, account or workspace deletion, and support. We aim to acknowledge requests within two business days.

This notice covers our hosted service. A self-hosted installation is operated by whoever runs it; their privacy and retention choices apply.

What we store and why

We store the memory and activity you or your agents submit, workspace names and settings, member email addresses, connection and access records, and billing references. We use these to run your workspace, authenticate people and agents, deliver sign-in and invitation emails, manage subscriptions, resolve support requests, and protect the service.

Memory access, memberships, and credentials are scoped to the workspace. Activity, billing, and access records in the shared control database are scoped to the workspace. People and agents in a shared workspace can read its shared data; a personal workspace is separate. Explicit sharing rules can copy selected content into another workspace. Workspace owners manage membership and agent access.

Engram observes calls to its own tools. Other agent actions appear when an agent or integration reports them. Connecting an agent does not automatically collect all of its conversations or tool use. A connected agent receives the workspace results it requests and handles them under its own service’s terms.

External AI is off by default

Core memory storage, keyword search, and activity history work without sending your content to an AI provider. A workspace owner can opt in to optional AI processing in Workspace settings.

When enabled and configured, TypeSafe can receive relevant memory, activity, and query context for analysis; Voyage can receive text for embeddings; and OpenAI can receive relevant activity context for optional generated briefs. Provider terms and retention apply to information they receive. Turning the setting off stops future optional processing; it does not recall data already sent. We do not promise zero retention or a particular training policy on behalf of another provider.

Hosting, payments, email, and cookies

Google Cloud hosts the service and database, and Cloudflare handles edge traffic. Hosting and security infrastructure may process IP addresses, request metadata, and diagnostic logs. The app’s bundled scripts do not include advertising pixels or third-party analytics; its scripts, icons, and fonts are served with the app.

A session cookie keeps you signed in. The browser can also keep non-sensitive interface preferences, such as dismissing the getting started guide. Agent access keys are shown when created and are not stored in browser storage by the app.

Stripe collects payment and billing information. Engram sends workspace/customer references to associate a subscription, without memory, activity content, or agent transcripts. Resend delivers hosted sign-in and invitation emails, including the recipient address and the message; invitations also identify the workspace and inviter. These services have their own privacy and retention terms.

Public sharing is a deliberate choice

Your activity profile starts unpublished. A workspace owner chooses its name, bio, and address before publishing. Public profiles contain aggregate activity counts, dates, and broad tool categories. They exclude private memory, agent names, action details, resource paths, and transcripts.

Unpublishing removes public access from Engram. It cannot recall copies already exported, shared to another workspace, or downloaded by social networks and other third parties.

How long information stays

Workspace memory, activity, and account/access records are retained until you request their removal. There is no automatic cleanup of inactive workspaces or activity history. Ending a trial or canceling a subscription does not erase your data; existing data remains readable and exportable.

Deleting a memory page in the app is recoverable soft deletion. It is not permanent erasure and does not remove associated activity, access records, or copies already shared elsewhere.

The hosted database keeps a rolling set of 14 automated backup copies and seven days of transaction logs for recovery. Fourteen backup copies is a count setting, not a guarantee that every deleted record disappears after 14 days. Earlier data can remain in recovery copies until they are replaced or removed. On-demand release/recovery backups are retained separately and can last longer.

The default Google Cloud application-log bucket is configured for 30 days of retention. This does not describe every security, system, or edge log, or records held by payment and email providers. We identify relevant retention exceptions when handling an erasure request.

Access, correction, and deletion requests

You can export the memory and activity your account can read from Workspace settings. Store the export carefully; it contains private data. For help accessing or correcting information, or to request account/workspace erasure, email ani@odstdio.com. Include your workspace name and account email, without tokens, passwords, or private memory contents.

There is no self-service account or workspace erasure button. An operator verifies your authority and processes the request manually, including the scope of shared-workspace information. Our target is to complete an approved request in the active service within 30 days after verification. We explain delays and any information retained for security, disputes, or accounting. Recovery copies expire or are removed separately; they are not used for ordinary workspace access. If we restore a backup, we reapply recorded erasure requests before returning restored data to service.

Security and self-hosting

The gateway must read workspace content to serve it, and an infrastructure administrator can access underlying storage and backups. Hosted data is not cryptographically isolated from the service operator. Engram does not promise absolute security, end-to-end encryption, or a compliance certification.

Self-hosting puts the infrastructure, backups, network security, and optional service keys under your operator’s control. See the self-hosting guide. For a security report, contact ani@odstdio.com privately; do not send live credentials or another person’s data.
